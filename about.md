Damask is an international vocal quartet, uniting four musicians of five
nationalities: American-Dutch soprano **Katharine Dain**, French
mezzo-soprano **Marine Fribourg**, English tenor **Guy Cutting**, and
Canadian baritone **Drew Santini**.

Since its founding in 2014, the ensemble has focused on the rich but
neglected repertoire written specifically for vocal quartet — chamber
works with piano by Schubert, Schumann, Brahms and Haydn, alongside a
cappella and instrumentally-accompanied music of the 20th and 21st
centuries by composers such as Milhaud, Messiaen, Stravinsky, Schönberg,
Ned Rorem and David Lang.

Commissioning new work is central to Damask's mission. The quartet has
premiered pieces by Gregory Spears, Reiko Füting, Lewis Nielson and
Federico Mosquera, and performed new arrangements by Matthijs van de
Woerd and Raphael Fusco, always written for — or adapted to — the
particular blend and versatility of the quartet's four voices.

## Recordings

In 2018 Damask released its debut album, **O schöne Nacht**, a program of
19th-century quartets by Brahms and his contemporaries Herzogenberg and
Jenner, alongside piano works by Kirchner — recorded with French pianist
Flore Merlin on a period Streicher instrument, in collaboration with
producer Frerik de Jong of 7 Mountain Records.

## Teaching

The members of Damask are committed teachers as well as performers, and
have given masterclasses for one-voice-per-part ensembles at the
Koorbiënnale in Haarlem (2017, 2019) and at the Conservatoire de Reims
(2023).

## The name

*Damask* refers to the luxurious woven textile motif developed in
14th-century Damascus — a vibrant crossing point of major trade routes
where materials, ideas, cultures and design practices moved freely between
peoples. The pattern, still in use today, is a fitting metaphor for an
ensemble that weaves together different repertoires, cultural traditions
and musical personalities into one strong, unified whole.
